> Is there a good curve or look up table people have figured out for DualSense 5 (for controlling rumble strength in a way that seems perceptually linear)? 

Yes, and because the DualSense actuators are essentially specialized audio speakers (Voice Coil Actuators), the most effective solution borrows heavily from audio engineering: **an "audio taper" or power curve.**

In game development, rather than hardcoding a massive, hand-tuned look-up table, the standard practice is to use a mathematical power function—similar to "gamma correction" in graphics—to generate a perceptually linear curve.

This counteracts the logarithmic nature of human touch (and the physics of the motor) by keeping the physical output very low for the first half of your input values, and ramping up aggressively at the end.

### The Math: The Power Curve

The most common and effective mapping function is:

$$y = x^\\gamma$$

Where $x$ is your linear input (from 0.0 to 1.0), and $\\gamma$ (gamma) is your exponent.

*   **$\\gamma = 1.0$ (Linear):** What you have now. 50% feels like 80%.
    
*   **$\\gamma = 2.0$ (Quadratic/Squared):** A great baseline for traditional spinning-weight (ERM) motors like Xbox controllers.
    
*   **$\\gamma = 2.5$ to $3.0$ (Cubic):** The sweet spot for the DualSense. Because it uses acoustic VCAs, it behaves like an audio speaker. A cubic curve closely mimics an audio volume slider, making fades from 0 to 100 feel perfectly smooth.

### Why this works so well

By using a LUT generated by a power curve, you dedicate the vast majority of your 256 "steps" of resolution to the lower end of the motor's power.

Because humans are incredibly sensitive to tiny vibrations but terrible at distinguishing between two very strong vibrations, this gives you massive precision where the player actually feels it, and ramps up the physical energy fast enough at the top end to satisfy the math required to feel "twice as strong."
